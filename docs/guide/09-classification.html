Chapter 10Classification: asking an AI the right question

The AI's only job is to read one posting and fill a strict form: where the person may live, which time zones, work authorization, seniority, and the sentence that proves it. Getting this step right is mostly about a precise prompt, a strict schema, and checking the answer.

Step 1: the pre-filter (no AI)

About half of all open jobs are clearly office-based. Sending them to the AI would waste free quota, so a regular-expression pre-filter skips them first:

{{lines pipeline/classify/prompt.py 43-55}} {{py pipeline/classify/prompt.py prefilter}}

It is conservative: a job is skipped only when nothing hints at remote work. A remote or hybrid flag, a remote word in the title or location, an entry-level title (interns often list only a city), or "remote" in the description all send it to the AI. An explicit office-only flag skips it. Skipped jobs are re-checked every run, so improving the pre-filter applies to old jobs too.

Step 2: the prompt

The system prompt defines every field, with examples and the traps found during testing. The most important rule: the location field is authoritative; company slogans like "we hire globally" do not make a US-only role worldwide.

{{lines pipeline/classify/prompt.py 5-12}}

Each posting is sent as a short, labelled message. Long descriptions are cut down by excerpt() to the opening plus every paragraph that mentions eligibility words (location, visa, time zone, contract…), at most 4,000 characters:

{{py pipeline/classify/prompt.py user_message}} {{py pipeline/classify/prompt.py excerpt}}

Step 3: the schema

The answer must match the Extraction model exactly. Validators clean it: country codes are upper-cased and filtered to two letters (and "UK" becomes "GB"), regions must come from a fixed list, the quote is trimmed to 200 characters.

{{py pipeline/classify/schema.py Extraction}}

The same structure is written out as a JSON Schema, sent with every request, so the model is forced to answer in that shape (Gemini's responseJsonSchema, OpenAI-style response_format: json_schema):

{{py pipeline/classify/schema.py JSON_SCHEMA}}
Structured output

Without a schema, a model might answer in prose, add fields, or misspell a value. With one, the provider constrains the generation so the output parses as JSON with the expected keys and enum values. Pydantic then validates it again on our side: belt and braces.

Step 4: checking the evidence

Models sometimes paraphrase or invent a quote. Every saved extraction is checked: the quote, normalised (lower case, punctuation removed), must appear in the posting. If not, confidence drops to low, and low confidence can never produce a firm "You can apply" (chapter 12).

{{py pipeline/classify/runner.py verify_evidence}}

Step 5: choosing what to send (triage)

{{py pipeline/classify/runner.py triage}}
  • Newest first (ORDER BY first_seen_at DESC): with a limited free quota, today's jobs matter most for alerts and Telegram.
  • Reuse: if an extraction with the same content_hash already exists (the same posting on two boards, or an unchanged repost), it is marked done without calling the AI.
  • One request per distinct content (todo.setdefault(job["content_hash"], job)).
