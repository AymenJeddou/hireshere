Chapter 12The verdict engine: rules, not AI

pipeline/classify/verdict.py answers one question with plain Python: given this extraction and this country, can someone living there apply? It runs 243 times per job at export time, costs nothing, and is fully tested.

The four verdicts

VerdictOn the siteWhen
eligible"You can apply" (green, filled pill)The posting names the country, or is explicitly open worldwide with role-level evidence.
likely"Likely open to you" (amber, half pill)A region that contains the country (EMEA for Tunisia), a time zone window that fits, an office in the country, or a low-confidence "eligible".
unclear"Ask the employer" (grey ring)Remote, but the posting does not say from where.
not_eligiblenot shownExcluded, limited elsewhere, needs work authorization elsewhere, wrong time zones, office elsewhere.

The decision, step by step

The order of the checks matters: the first rule that applies wins, and the strongest "no" signals come first.

{{py pipeline/classify/verdict.py decide}}
  1. Explicitly excluded ("not available in X") beats everything.
  2. Office or hybrid: likely if the office is in this country, otherwise not eligible.
  3. Work authorization required elsewhere ("must be authorized to work in the US"): not eligible unless this country is in the list.
  4. The country is named: eligible (capped to likely if confidence is low).
  5. A list of other countries: not eligible.
  6. Worldwide: check the time zones, check that "worldwide" describes this role and not the company, and that the title or location do not list regions; then eligible.
  7. Regions: likely if one contains the country, otherwise not eligible.
  8. Time zone only: likely if the window overlaps the country's offsets.
  9. Otherwise unclear.

The helpers

Time zone overlap

{{py pipeline/classify/verdict.py _tz_ok}}

Two ranges [a, b] and [c, d] overlap when c <= b and a <= d. A posting asking for "CET plus or minus 2 hours" (UTC-1 to UTC+3) fits Tunisia (UTC+1) and Egypt (UTC+2), but not the US.

Company slogan or role rule?

"We are a work-from-anywhere company" says nothing about who this particular job hires. Before trusting a worldwide extraction, the code looks for role-level evidence in the location field or the quote:

{{py pipeline/classify/verdict.py _role_level_worldwide}} {{py pipeline/classify/verdict.py _cap}}

Reasons in three languages

decide() returns a reason key and parameters, never a sentence. reason() renders the sentence in English, French or Arabic. French templates never put an article or preposition before a country name ("{name_fr} figure parmi…"), so they are correct for "Tunisie", "Maroc" and "États-Unis" alike.

{{py pipeline/classify/verdict.py reason}}
Try it

Change one rule (for example, make regions give "eligible") and run uv run pytest tests/test_classify.py -q: the tests show exactly which real cases the change breaks. Because verdicts are computed at export time, a rule change needs no AI calls; the next export applies it to every job.
