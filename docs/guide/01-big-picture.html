Chapter 2The idea and the big picture

Most "remote" jobs are remote only inside one country or region. HiresHere reads each posting once, works out who may apply, and shows every visitor only the jobs open to where they live, with the sentence from the posting that proves it.

The problem in one example

A posting titled "Senior Data Engineer, Remote" may say, halfway down: "You must be based in the EU and authorized to work there." Someone in Tunisia reads the title, spends an evening on the application, and never hears back. Multiply that by hundreds of postings a week. HiresHere's job is to find that sentence for every posting and turn it into a clear answer per country.

The pipeline in six steps

  1. Fetch. Read the public job feeds of 537 company boards on 7 ATS platforms, plus the Remote OK and Jobicy feeds. Store every open job in SQLite. Mark jobs that disappeared as expired.
  2. Pre-filter. Skip jobs that are clearly office-only, so the AI is not spent on them.
  3. Extract. Ask an AI model to read each remaining posting once and fill a strict schema: who may apply, from where, in which time zones, at what seniority, with an evidence quote.
  4. Decide. Plain Python rules turn one extraction into a verdict for each of 243 countries. No AI here, so adding a country costs nothing.
  5. Export and publish. Write compact JSON files and build a static website with Astro; deploy it to Cloudflare Pages.
  6. Notify. Email subscribers a daily digest of new jobs open to their country, and post to Telegram channels.
Data flows left to right along the top, then back down to the visitor. GitHub Actions runs the whole chain once a day.

Three ideas that shape everything

1. Read each posting once, decide many times

The expensive, slow step is the AI reading a posting. So the AI produces a country-neutral extraction once per posting (stored by content hash), and cheap deterministic rules turn that into a verdict for any number of countries. When HiresHere went from 4 countries to 243, no posting had to be re-read.

2. Cautious by design

A wrong "You can apply" wastes someone's evening and destroys trust; an honest "Ask the employer" costs little. Every rule leans cautious, low-confidence AI answers are capped at "likely", and the evidence quote must really appear in the posting or the confidence drops.

3. Free tiers only

The whole system runs on free plans: GitHub Actions (free for public repositories), Cloudflare Pages, Functions and D1, Resend's free email plan, the free tiers of Gemini, Groq and Mistral. Many design choices (static pages, data shards, request pacing, one digest a day) exist to stay inside those limits.

What a visitor sees

  • A search page with a country select in the header. Each job card shows one verdict pill for that country ("You can apply", "Likely open to you", "Ask the employer"), a short reason, and the date the employer posted it.
  • A job page that opens with "You can apply from Brazil" (or the honest alternative), the reason, the exact quote, a world map coloured by verdict, and links to apply on the employer's site.
  • Company pages, a getting-paid guide, an employer form, saved jobs and an application tracker kept in the browser, all in English, French and Arabic (right to left).
  • Optional daily email alerts and per-country Telegram channels.
